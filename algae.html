How to Prevent & Treat Algae

Overview

A note on this guide: Algae is the #1 reason people get frustrated and quit this hobby. So I'm going to share everything I know — every tip, every tool, every thing I would do. It's the most thorough framework I can give you.

The truth is, algae isn't a complicated problem. Like most things, prevention and catching it early give you the best chance of success. And there's a lot you can do on both fronts — which is exactly what this guide covers.

The Right Way to Think About It

Algae doesn't take over a healthy tank. It takes over a struggling one.

Think of it like a compromised immune system. The healthier your plants, the stronger their resistance — and the larger your margin of error when something goes slightly wrong. An extra feeding, a fish that died somewhere you can't see, a slightly off water change. Healthy plants absorb the hit. Struggling plants don't.

Healthier plants = less algae

The 3 Variables

Every planted tank is controlled by three things: CO2, light, and nutrients. My general approach from day one is to take nutrients out of the equation entirely, keep light on the lower side, and run CO2 significantly higher than average. Fewer variables means less to troubleshoot — and less that can go wrong.

Treatment

Algae in week one or two is completely normal — don't panic. But if you're still dealing with it by week four, it's time to move to active treatment.

When algae does appear, the approach is layered. Start with the least invasive steps first and add more based on how serious the problem is. Whatever combination you run, give it a minimum of two weeks — ideally three — before judging whether it's working.

If you want to pinpoint the exact cause, split test one change at a time. If you just want the problem gone, run the full framework for your severity level and let it work.
