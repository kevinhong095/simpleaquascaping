Best Practices for Lights

  • Buy more powerful lights than you need, then run them dimmer. This gives you better control and extends the lifespan of your lights.
  • Avoid relying on light apps for scheduling — most are unintuitive and prone to errors.
  • Use a smart plug for timing. Set your desired color in the app, leave the light "always on," and control on/off with a smart plug for a simpler, more reliable setup.
  • Skip the sunrise/sunset ramp features. Gradual brightness changes have minimal real impact and add unnecessary complexity.

How Many Lights Do You Need?

45–90cm tanks1 light
120cm+ tanks2 lights
  • A single light can work on a 90cm tank if it's low-tech or low-demand
  • Two lights are preferred on 90cm if you're running higher light or more demanding plants

Best Practices for CO2

  • Use an inline CO2 diffuser. It injects CO2 directly into your filter outflow — more efficient than in-tank diffusers and keeps your tank visually cleaner.
  • Run CO2 as high as your tank can safely handle. This helps prevent and control algae. In most cases, that's roughly 2–4 BPS.
  • I don't use drop checkers — they're slow and misleading. I push CO2 as high as possible. If fish are fine, you're fine.

How to Dial It In

  1. 1
    Start at 1 bubble per second (BPS).
  2. 2
    Increase by +1 BPS every 3 days.

Watch your fish

If they begin gasping at the surface → stop CO2 for the day. The next day, resume at the last stable BPS.
